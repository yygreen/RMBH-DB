Where we stand

A library has been built

The last engagement produced 503 pieces of work — 414 articles arranged under 17 hubs and 46 sub-hubs across 20 subject pillars, including 319 prayers set out properly in Hebrew, transliteration and English, and an eighty-page account of Rabbi Meir Baal Haness that has no equal in English. This plan builds on that, not in place of it.

Traffic from Google has declined over the past six months. That is reported openly on the dashboard, and establishing what sits behind it is work for the first phase — more than one thing is likely involved, and the site’s two measurement tools currently disagree about the size of it. We would rather find out than guess. The case for everything below rests instead on four things that can be checked today.

The case

Four things that can be checked today

One

The site ranks for a great deal it does not capture

175 searches already sit between fourth and tenth place — seen by the searcher, rarely chosen. Twenty more, worth some 24,000 enquiries a month, sit on the second page, and yahrzeit, kaddish and the mourning terms account for twelve thousand of those alone. The pages exist and the rankings exist; what is missing is the last few places.
→ Page Enhancement & Depth. Not new titles — the sections readers look for and do not currently find, so the page is worth the higher position.
Two

The ask is everywhere. The means to act is not

A typical page carries around ninety giving links and the wording is good — “Accompany Your Prayer with Tzedakah,” “Give in Memory of What He Preserved.” What separates the pages that earn is simpler than persuasion: whether the giving form sits on the page itself. Forty-five per cent carry one. The rest ask, then send the reader elsewhere to act.
→ The Giving Path. Extend the pattern that already works on your best pages to those that draw the most readers, matched to each subject.
Three

The record is connected, but does not agree with itself

The footer already links out to GuideStar, Charity Navigator, PayPal Giving Fund and both app stores, and those profiles link back. That part was built properly. The difficulty is what they say: the name is written differently on nearly every one, several sit unfinished with sections a careful donor reads left blank, and the site’s code does not yet declare which profiles belong to it.
→ Name, Record & Trust. One legal name and one public name, corrected everywhere, the profiles completed and the connection declared in code.
Four

Ask an AI assistant, and it names somebody else

Twenty questions go to the assistants each month — which charity gives in the merit of Rabbi Meir, where a kvittel can be sent to the kever. RMBH is named in one of the twenty. Others appear more often because their record is easier for a machine to resolve. This is measured by asking, so it owes nothing to search traffic either way.
→ Outside Recognition, with Recordings and the entity work. Be the easiest record to resolve, and the most cited.

Five hundred, line by line

Ten lines of work, each with its share of the five hundred. Every deliverable is logged on this dashboard as it ships.

100
Page Enhancement & Depth
100 of 500

The 175 searches already ranking between fourth and tenth are the cheapest ground on the site. But the work goes further than titles and links. Each page is given the sections readers look for and do not currently find — the fuller text, the practical answer, the halachic detail, the related tefillos, the natural next thing to read. A deeper page holds a reader longer and leads somewhere, and both of those are things a search engine can see. No page is rewritten from scratch and every page keeps its address.

For example
the prayer for lost objects page, the kvittel page, the hachnosas kallah page, the parnassah page and the Rabbi Meir collection — each given the missing section, a clearer answer in the opening lines, this year’s amounts, a visible review date, and a proper path onward to the next page
75
Occasion & Demand Pages
75 of 500

New pages where people are already searching and already giving. The largest single gap is mourning and remembrance: yahrzeit, kaddish and memorial searches run to roughly twelve thousand a month, every one of them a moment when giving in someone’s memory is part of what a family does — and the site sits on the second page of Google for all of them. Lifecycle moments come next, then the fixed dates of the calendar.

For example
the Mourner’s Kaddish and why it is said for eleven months, when to light a yahrzeit candle, what to give in a parent’s memory and how the wording is done, a bris, an upsherin, a pidyon haben, matanos l’evyonim on Purim morning, kimcha d’pischa before Pesach, the 14 Iyar yahrzeit

Recordings, Video & Printables
65 of 500

Prayers recorded properly and posted with the Hebrew, the transliteration and the English on screen — published to a YouTube channel of our own and embedded on the page each one belongs to, with the full text written out beneath so that search engines and AI assistants can read what is being said. A page carrying audio or video holds a reader far longer than a page of text alone, and the channel itself becomes another place the organisation is recognised. Printable one-page sheets alongside, each in two forms.

For example
Elokah d’Meir aneini for a lost object, Tefillas HaShelah for children, Tehillim 121 before a journey, the tefillah said at the kever in Teveria, a refuah sheet with a line for the choleh’s name, the kvittel form to send to Teveria, a Tefillas HaDerech card for the car

The Giving Path
50 of 500

Forty-five per cent of the site’s pages already take a gift in place — the Guarantee page, the kvittel page, find a lost object, parnassah, a bris, a pidyon haben. Those are the pages that earn. The rest ask just as well and then hand the reader off. This line extends the pattern that already works to the pages drawing the most readers, matched to the subject each page is about, and brings the giving amounts into line across every route including the outside platforms.

For example
a giving form placed on the Rabbi Meir collection and the legacy giving pages, a yahrzeit page that takes a memorial gift in the niftar’s name without leaving the page, a refuah page that sends the choleh’s name to Teveria, chai amounts carried through to the outside platforms, and a same-day alert if a form ever fails

Name, Record & Trust
50 of 500

This settles on one legal name and one public name, corrects them everywhere they appear, declares in the site’s code which outside profiles belong to the organisation, and completes the trust profiles a careful donor reads before giving. It also builds the page nobody has written: a fully identified account of the organisation — legal name, tax record, ruling year, address, rabbinical oversight and programmes — stated identically wherever it appears, so that a donor and a search engine both land on the same organisation without ambiguity.

For example
the four Charity Navigator sections completed, the Candid profile finished and its transparency seal earned, the PayPal Giving Fund record corrected, the LinkedIn page linked up like the others, the tax record and founding history declared in code, a Wikidata entry created, and an identity page carrying the legal name, tax record and rabbinical oversight, matching the outside records exactly

Outside Recognition
40 of 500

The organisation is mentioned and linked from roughly a third as many places as the charities it is measured against, and that gap is the clearest reason it is not yet recognised as the authority on its own name. This pursues the mentions that carry weight — Jewish and Israeli publications, resource lists, charity directories, and the kind of citation a search engine and an AI assistant both treat as a vote. Nothing bought, nothing artificial.

For example
a piece around the 14 Iyar yahrzeit, resource listings for the printable prayer sheets and the recordings, the Rabbi Meir collection cited by sites that currently have nothing to point to, directory profiles completed, and inclusion in the charity lists donors consult in Elul

Helpful Content & Tools
35 of 500

Short, genuinely useful things a person comes back for and forwards to someone else — a real answer in the first three lines, a date worked out for them, a checklist they can follow. These are the shape of answer the AI assistants tend to quote, and they are the reason a visitor opens a second page instead of leaving after the first.

For example
how much machatzis hashekel is this year, a yahrzeit date converter, what to write in a kvittel, whether a woman can send one, a shiva and mourning timeline, a Tehillim finder for a given situation, and where pushka money actually goes

Technical & Publishing Advisory
35 of 500

Our access is a staging environment without caching, so changes to the live site are made by your developer. This line is therefore the diagnosis, the written specification and the verification afterwards — the work that lets your team ship quickly and safely, rather than a promise we are not the ones holding. It also covers the templates and publishing order for the pages already written and waiting.

For example
the oversized homepage image identified and a replacement supplied, retired tracking tags listed for removal, redirect chains mapped, page-type code blocks written ready to drop in, a giving-path block your developer can reuse, a publishing order putting highest demand first, and verification once each is live
